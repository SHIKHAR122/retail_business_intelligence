
# Module 2: Sales Aggregation & Profitability Analysis

## 1. Objective

This module focuses on aggregating transactional sales data to generate business-level performance insights. The goal is to analyze revenue distribution, profitability patterns, and identify underperforming segments using structured SQL analysis.

The module transforms row-level transactional data into strategic performance metrics across categories and sub-categories.

---

## 2. Dataset Overview

Source Table: `superstore_clean`

Key Columns Used:

* `category`
* `sub_category`
* `sales`
* `profit`
* `region`
* `segment`
* `order_date`

The dataset contains transactional sales records, which are aggregated to derive category-level and sub-category-level insights.

---

## 3. Completed Analyses

### 3.1 Total Sales by Category

Aggregated total revenue per category to identify primary revenue drivers within the business.

### 3.2 Total Profit by Category

Computed cumulative profit across categories to assess profitability distribution.

### 3.3 Profit Margin by Category

Calculated profit margin (Total Profit ÷ Total Sales) to evaluate operational efficiency across categories.

This enables identification of:

* High-efficiency categories
* Revenue-heavy but low-margin categories

### 3.4 Revenue Contribution Percentage

Measured each category’s contribution to overall company revenue to determine dependency concentration.

Business Insight:
Identifies categories that dominate revenue share and highlights over-reliance risks.

### 3.5 Ranking Categories by Revenue

Used window functions to rank categories by total revenue, enabling structured performance comparison.

### 3.6 Ranking Categories by Profit

Ranked categories by total profit to identify the most value-generating business segments.

### 3.7 Loss-Making Sub-Category Detection

Aggregated sub-category performance and identified segments with negative total profit.

This highlights:

* Structural inefficiencies
* Pricing or cost management issues
* Potential candidates for strategic review

---

## 4. Analytical Techniques Applied

* GROUP BY Aggregations
* Common Table Expressions (CTEs)
* Window Functions (DENSE_RANK)
* HAVING Clause Filtering
* Ratio Calculations (Profit Margin)
* Revenue Contribution Analysis
* Severity Ranking of Loss Segments

This module demonstrates structured SQL design separating aggregation logic from analytical ranking logic.

---

## 5. Preliminary Business Observations

* Revenue concentration is uneven across categories.
* Some high-revenue categories exhibit relatively low profit margins.
* Certain sub-categories operate at net losses despite generating sales volume.

These findings indicate potential pricing inefficiencies and cost structure imbalances.

# 6. Sales Aggregation by Region

## 6.1 Objective

To evaluate geographic revenue distribution and assess regional business performance using aggregated sales and profit metrics.

The goal is to move beyond raw revenue comparison and incorporate profitability awareness into regional evaluation.

---

## 6.2 Regional Sales Aggregation

Transactional records were aggregated at the region level to compute:

* Total Sales per Region
* Total Profit per Region
* Profit Margin per Region

This transformation converts row-level sales data into region-level performance summaries suitable for strategic comparison.

---

## 6.3 Highest and Lowest Performing Regions

Regions were compared based on total sales to identify:

* Highest-performing region (maximum total revenue)
* Lowest-performing region (minimum total revenue)

Tie scenarios were handled by comparing against global maximum and minimum aggregated values to ensure accurate classification.

This approach avoids bias introduced by simple row ordering and ensures analytical consistency.

---

## 6.4 Sales vs Profit Mismatch Detection

Regional performance was evaluated using both revenue and profitability metrics.

Mismatch conditions identified:

* High Sales + Low Profit Margin
  → Indicates possible pricing inefficiencies, discount pressure, or elevated cost structures.

* Low Sales + High Profit Margin
  → Indicates niche profitability or under-leveraged growth potential.

This dual-metric evaluation prevents misleading conclusions based solely on revenue magnitude.

---

## 6.5 Regional Performance Labeling

Regions were categorized into performance types using conditional classification logic:

* Highest Performer
* Lowest Performer
* High Sales – Low Margin (Risk Zone)
* Low Sales – High Margin (Opportunity Zone)
* Normal Performance

This structured labeling enables easier downstream reporting and dashboard visualization.

---

# 7. Analytical Design Principles Applied

This module follows a layered SQL design:

1. Base Aggregation Layer
   → Summarizes transactional data into region-level and category-level metrics.

2. Metric Computation Layer
   → Calculates derived indicators such as profit margin and revenue contribution.

3. Comparative Layer
   → Applies ranking, min/max comparison, and average benchmarking.

4. Classification Layer
   → Labels performance categories for strategic interpretation.

This modular structure ensures scalability and supports future analytical expansion.

---

# 8. Business Interpretation – Regional Analysis

Key strategic implications from regional aggregation:

* Revenue distribution is not necessarily aligned with profitability distribution.
* A high-revenue region may operate with thin margins, indicating operational inefficiency.
* Lower-revenue regions with strong margins may represent expansion opportunities.
* Performance evaluation must incorporate both volume and efficiency metrics.

This reinforces the importance of multi-metric evaluation in business intelligence workflows.
