
# Module 2: Sales Aggregation & Profitability Analysis

---

## 1. Objective

This module focuses on aggregating transactional sales data to generate business-level performance insights. The goal is to analyze revenue distribution, profitability patterns, and identify underperforming segments using structured SQL analysis.

The module transforms row-level transactional data into strategic performance metrics across:

* Category
* Sub-Category
* Region
* Segment
* Time (Monthly & Seasonal)

---

## 2. Dataset Overview

**Source Table:** `superstore_clean`

### Key Columns Used:

* `category`
* `sub_category`
* `region`
* `segment`
* `sales`
* `profit`
* `order_id`
* `order_date`

The dataset contains transactional sales records, which are aggregated to derive multi-level business insights.

---

# 3. Category-Level Aggregation

### 3.1 Total Sales by Category

Aggregated total revenue per category to identify primary revenue drivers.

### 3.2 Total Profit by Category

Computed cumulative profit across categories to assess profitability distribution.

### 3.3 Profit Margin by Category

Calculated:

Profit Margin = Total Profit ÷ Total Sales

Used to evaluate operational efficiency.

### 3.4 Revenue Contribution Percentage

Measured each category’s contribution to overall revenue.

### 3.5 Ranking Categories by Revenue

Used window functions (`DENSE_RANK`) for structured comparison.

### 3.6 Ranking Categories by Profit

Ranked categories by total profit to identify value-generating units.

### 3.7 Loss-Making Sub-Category Detection

Identified sub-categories with negative total profit to highlight structural inefficiencies.

---

# 4. Regional Sales Aggregation

## 4.1 Objective

Evaluate geographic revenue distribution and assess regional performance using both revenue and profitability metrics.

## 4.2 Regional Metrics Computed

* Total Sales per Region
* Total Profit per Region
* Profit Margin per Region

## 4.3 Highest and Lowest Performing Regions

Regions were classified using aggregated maximum and minimum comparisons to avoid row-order bias.

## 4.4 Sales vs Profit Mismatch Detection

Identified:

* High Sales + Low Margin (Risk Zone)
* Low Sales + High Margin (Opportunity Zone)

## 4.5 Regional Performance Labeling

Regions categorized into:

* Highest Performer
* Lowest Performer
* Risk Zone
* Opportunity Zone
* Normal Performance

---

# 5. Segment-Based Sales & Profitability Analysis

## 5.1 Objective

Analyze customer segments to determine revenue concentration, profitability efficiency, and value contribution across customer types.

## 5.2 Segment Metrics Computed

### 5.2.1 Total Sales by Segment

Aggregated total revenue per segment.

### 5.2.2 Total Profit by Segment

Calculated cumulative profit generated by each segment.

### 5.2.3 Profit Margin by Segment

Profit Margin = Total Profit ÷ Total Sales

Distinguishes revenue-heavy but low-efficiency segments from high-efficiency segments.

### 5.2.4 Revenue Contribution % by Segment

Measured each segment’s percentage contribution to total company revenue.

### 5.2.5 Average Order Value (AOV) by Segment

Average Order Value = Total Sales ÷ Total Orders

Evaluates purchasing intensity and transactional behavior.

### 5.2.6 Ranking Segments by Revenue

Used `DENSE_RANK` to compare revenue performance.

### 5.2.7 Ranking Segments by Profitability

Ranked segments by total profit generated.

---

# 6. Monthly Sales Summary (Time-Based Analysis)

## 6.1 Objective

Introduce temporal performance evaluation by aggregating sales data on a monthly basis.

This marks the transition from static structural analysis to dynamic trend analysis.

---

## 6.2 Monthly Metrics Computed

* Monthly Total Sales (Company-Level)
* Monthly Total Profit
* Monthly Profit Margin
* Monthly Sales by Segment
* Month-over-Month (MoM) Growth %
* Monthly Sales Volatility

---

## 6.3 Analytical Purpose

Monthly aggregation enables:

* Detection of short-term fluctuations
* Revenue acceleration or deceleration tracking
* Identification of peak and weak periods
* Stability vs volatility assessment
* Early detection of performance shifts

---

## 6.4 Techniques Applied

* Date Extraction (Year–Month formatting)
* GROUP BY monthly period
* Window Functions (`LAG`) for MoM Growth
* Growth Rate Computation
* Trend Classification

---

# 7. Seasonal Pattern Detection (Next Phase)

## 7.1 Objective

Move beyond simple monthly aggregation to identify **recurring seasonal behavior** in sales and profit performance.

The goal is to detect cyclical demand patterns that repeat annually or periodically.

---

## 7.2 Analytical Motivation

Monthly numbers alone show movement.
Seasonal detection shows **repeatable structure**.

Seasonality analysis helps identify:

* High-demand months
* Consistently low-performing months
* Festival/holiday-driven spikes
* End-of-quarter effects
* Business cycle dependencies

This elevates the analysis from trend tracking to pattern intelligence.

---

## 7.3 Planned Seasonal Metrics

* Average Sales by Month (Across Years)
* Average Profit by Month
* Seasonal Revenue Index
* Seasonal Profit Index
* Peak Season Identification
* Off-Season Identification
* Segment-Level Seasonal Variation

---

## 7.4 Seasonal Analytical Techniques

* Extract Month from `order_date`
* Multi-Year Monthly Aggregation
* Normalization (Monthly Average ÷ Overall Monthly Mean)
* Variance Analysis
* Standard Deviation Across Months
* Pattern Stability Testing

---

## 7.5 Business Applications

Seasonal intelligence supports:

* Inventory Planning
* Marketing Campaign Timing
* Discount Optimization
* Capacity Planning
* Budget Forecasting

# 9. Business Observations (Consolidated)

Across category, region, segment, and time-based analyses:

* Revenue concentration is uneven across business dimensions.
* High revenue does not guarantee high profitability.
* Certain segments operate at scale but with thinner margins.
* Regional inefficiencies exist despite strong revenue.
* Monthly performance shows volatility patterns.
* Emerging seasonal structures suggest cyclical demand behavior.

These findings demonstrate the importance of layered and multi-dimensional evaluation.
